# Stellar Blade

_August 10, 2026_

<p>
    <img class="img-logo snes-container no-padding" src="/images/sb-logo.png">
</p>

_May your memories live on forever._

### First Impression

Since seeing the trailers years and years ago, Stellar Blade has been on my wishlist. I never necessarily wanted to buy it on PS5, and lucky for me the game ended up launching on PC a couple years down the road. I had a hard time justifying the full price tag, even when it came to PC as I just wasn't convinced the gameplay was truly there, considering all the "gameplay" anyone really showed on social media were...risque to say the least. Is Stellar Blade a suggestive game? Yes, the camera often follows the protagonist Eve in such a way to make spectacle of her body, even when everything around you is just a typical action game setting. Is that the only thing the game should be known for? No, turns out that game that all of your friends were side eyeing you for ended up being a lot of fun, with a lot of skill expression, and a sci-fi world rich with story worth investing time into.

### Story

To be honest, I wasn't really expecting much in terms of story in this game. It's always good to be pleasantly surprised, And I think stellar blade satisfied in that regard. I wasn't necessarily hooked until you find the human civilization a couple of missions in to the game. From there you meet a character that I think was particularly shady, and had me guessing their Motive for the entire game. Not like this game's going to win Any BAFTA awards for its story, I do feel like it was serviceable enough to provide a few thought provoking moments, and satisfying twist That resolves well in the end. Eve is a good protagonist, however I feel like the story was mostly driven by the side cast, and the missions that weren't integral to the main story were the ones I ended up caring about the most. There were moments for me where you truly felt like a guardian Angel, helping the people of Xion maintain a shred of hope in the dull world around them. I was happy with the main plot beats of the story, from traveling to the different regions, to the "dungeons" of each new region that gave more insight to what was happening in the world. Despite a lot of the story coming from notes and items found in the game, much like souls like games, I do feel the story presenting itself to you in a typical action RPG fashion works better here then solely relying on in game lore to drive the story. I'm glad there was a cast of characters to enjoy, and I'm glad to be surprised.

### Look & Feel

Now say what you will about games from Korea, especially now since the gaming scene over there is really starting to take off, I do think they have... a look. I can't really put my finger on what that look necessarily is, But a variety of games from Korea have it, the first descendant, Aion, Lies of P, Stellar Blade. It's not a bad thing, It's just something I noticed as I started expanding what I played into South Korean games. Anyway, Stella Blade looks fantastic. Shift up deliberately chose to develop Stellar Blade on Unreal Engine 4 for the performance boost. In an era with so many Unreal Engine five games looking like a blurry mess, I really appreciate how crisp the picture is in stellar blade. Not to mention the game runs exceptionally well, despite my older PC And despite it Being a fairly new game. I think the character design is quite good, what people wear and how they look is reflective of The post apocalyptic sci fi look they're going for. The world's resigned quite well, and I really like the dystopian look kind of paired with that post apocalyptic look, It gives something unique that you don't really see in too many games - Probably the most notable for me that's a similar vibe is Rage which I played last year. I really loved the enemy design looks, I thought the Naytiba were all pretty unique - reminding me a lot of some floor forms from halo lol. Bosses were also quite good, although most of them were kind of based on creatures from Earth- Which is fitting for some of the reveals in the story. I started to get a little bit exhausted of the Naytiba towards the half way point, But Stellar Blade does a good job of introducing An interesting enemies throughout the length of my playthrough.

I did really enjoy the gameplay. I feel like it felt similar to lies of P in a way, Where you're almost kind of meant to stand your ground, Sekiro style, And deflect a ton of attacks to find an opening. In fact the gameplay is designed around it, With a stagger system that rewards you with a large punishing attack after X amount of successful perfect parries. I did have to get an exo-spine to open that window a little more to be a little more forgiving, but the fact that the exo spine mechanic is here, allows for unique tailoring for different playstyles. Now as far as build variety goes, There are no other weapons you use outside of your original kit, So the build crafting all comes from which pieces of the kit you use the most, and building to min Max your playstyle. For example for me I really like to use the beta skills, and so I specked out different upgrades to assist me in getting more beta charge from killing enemies, Getting more beta charged for being hit, And going into a trance when using my beta energy so that I can keep using beta energy for free. This ended up sort of breaking the game towards the end, allowing me to stun lock bosses, and play really without needing to deflect at all, which was a ton of fun. Now while it was fun, I feel like I kind of found a broken build and potentially took some of the challenge out of the game. I do wish That stellar blade was a bit more challenging, especially because death is kind of weightless- Meaning when you die you just go back to the last checkpoint. No loss, no risk, No reward. It's one of those little things I think that set this on a stage a little bit lower than some of my other favorite games in this genre.

### Sound & Music

In general the sound and music in stellar Blade Varies from ear wormy to almost unnoticeable. There are so many musical tracks in the game that some of them, especially the bosses, kind of blur into one another. What I'm finding is that with a lot of action RPGS/souls likes I'm really starting to get exhausted of the Big Horn Big Boss motif. In a fantasy game like Elden Ring it works, but the tracks to me that stick out even amongst those games are the ones that are the most different. What's that stellar blade apart from a lot of these other games is that it has exploration music. Now it's all sung in Korean, So to me it ends up kind of just sounding like K drama soundtrack, But that doesn't mean it's bad. It fills the empty space in the large open areas, It makes it feel a little bit less lonely. What I did love was some of the safe room music, because it's just straight K pop- and the one that I used called blur Made me look forward to going back to save. A similar feeling to the safe room music in the Surge. There are a few tracks on the soundtrack that I really did enjoy, that were a little more EDM forward and fit the urgentness of when they appeared in the story. Sound is fine, don't really have many comments on that one outside of hearing Eve yell "wuaaaahhh" after nearly every combo did get a bit annoying at times, but I digress.

### Fun Factor

Stellar blade, fun factor I pretty much knew I was gonna have a good time with stellar blade. Action RPG sci-fi dystopian world and reasons to actually explore. On top of that put in combo based. and Perry based combat and I’m hooked, I think the mechanics of the game were gated in such a way to keep it interesting as you move throughout the story well also giving you more and more tools to play with classic game design done well and I really appreciated it for it. I had a lot of fun with the customization as well despite it being mostly swimsuits lol I was able to customize Eve into an evangelion like plug suit, which really cemented her character as part of the world for me. I think the bosses were fun and rewarding, but not as rewarding as other souls games because you just lose nothing on death so there is no fear for dying over and over again something in my mind tells me. that a lot of these small nuances in the first game can be ironed out and perfected in the second game and that makes me really excited to play the sequel.

### Overall

After about 40 hours, completing about 90%, and getting the true ending in the game- I think it's pretty fair to say that I had a fun time. I liked the combat, I liked the world and the story, I liked the side missions, And I like the traversal (despite being difficult to control at times, especially when entering the open world "boost" mode). It's not really a game that I wouldn't recommend to someone. There's a lot here for everyone. Do you like sci fi action games? Get it. Do you like female protagonists like Bayonetta? Get it. Do you like dystopian post apocalyptic settings? Get it. Do you like dressing up your main character, and tailoring them to be who they sound like in your mind? Get it. Stellar Blade is a great game Without a doubt. Clearly a lot of love was poured into this game and it shows. I'm really happy That shift up announced to sequel this year, Because I will be buying that day one.

# Croc: Legend of the Gobbos

_August 17, 2026_

<p>
    <img class="img-logo snes-container no-padding" src="/images/croc-logo.png">
</p>

_Yazoo! Shazam!_

### First Impression

A wave of nostalgia started by a great opening sequence in a very typical 90s bedroom. I was ready to rehash some early childhood memories with Croc. Upon entering I could tell a lot of love went into the original game, and all the extras to give you a glimpse into the development is such a nice touch. It really feels like this game being ported over to modern hardware was to be able to have more people share the experience. The modernized movement was a super nice touch, and really makes jumping in to play this easier than ever before.

### Story

Theres not much in terms of story here, like many 90s games. Croc is adopted by the Gobbos, and spends his childhood with them growing up. Suddenly, Mr Evil Bad Guy™ shows up and kidnaps all the Gobbos and its up to Croc to save them! You then spend the rest of the game romping through a variety of levels saving said Gobbos, and thwarting any bosses that get thrown your way.

### Look & Feel

Not much was changed in terms of looks. Croc gets a nice HD texturing, better lighting effects, and no more texture warble, but overall everything looks nearly identical - really selling the nostalgia. The movement being changed from the tank controls to omnidirectional with the joystick was probably one of the biggest improvements, and I can tell some of the sections must have been agonizing without it back in the day. If you played Croc on the PS1, playing now feels like a treat, instead of like a chore.

### Sound & Music

Now for some reason, I dont remember Croc having any music when I was a kid. Not sure if I turned it off, or if my disc was scratched or what, but I swear it was all atmospheric, which too me really identified Croc in my mind. However, I was actually disappointed this was not the case! Not that the music is bad, just compared to other games at the time, Crocs music is really...underwhelming? I even turned it off for a spell because I felt it was very repetitive at times - an often hallmark of some of these older games.

### Fun Factor

Croc is fun, and its fun to rehash some memories by playing through it again on modern hardware. I do think there was a reason I never beat it as a kid, and the same reason why I am not beating it (at least right now) as an adult. The games level design is just old. A lot of precise timing, positioning, and unforgiving checkpoints to artificially lengthen Crocs otherwise 3-4 hour playtime. Especially without save states, the frustration of losing all your lives to a difficult section and having to start the level over is not something someone who plays games for fun has time to truly spend time getting over. I know its an old game, and I think the oldness doesn't do it any favors in this instance.

### Overall

I wish I could've seen Croc through to the end. Its obvious it was, and still is a labor of love by the developers. I think that despite everything, Croc is showing its nearly 30 year old age, and often not in good ways. This is great fun to have for the first few worlds, but towards the end of the game, the difficulty spike can be overly frustrating, and I deemed it not worth my time past a few hours of fun. My rating is much lower than I wanted so I am going to also give a secondary rating on how I felt actually playing except for the end. I feel like this with multiple games, they just leave a bad end taste in my mouth that affects the score. Croc IS worth playing, especially if you played when you were younger, and this is the way to play it outside of the original hardware.

# Psychonauts

_August 19, 2026_

<p>
	<img class="img-logo snes-container no-padding" src="/images/psychonauts-logo.png">
</p>

_Eh, eh eh, tsk, eh eh, eh eh, tsk_

### First Impression

Ah, I remember this game. I remember it fondly. A journey through the mind, exploring ideas like depression, rage, mental blocking, paranoia, all disguised as a "kids" game. One thing I wish now in hindsight, is I wish I just had the original copy on the Xbox, because man...what a pain in the ass to get this working on modern hardware, especially the steam machine. Not only did I have to download and run scripts, I still never fixed the broken PC icons for the actions in game. Didn't really hinder the experience, but it did leave me a little worried for my run time with it. Luckily after the initial setup, it ran like a dream (despite the button icon issue), and for a 20 year old game, holds up remarkably well thanks to its amazing writing, art style, and timeless game design. There is a reason everyone has heard of Psychonauts, and once it was setup, I was itching to play.

### Story

Rasputin is the name, a gifted child psychic who ran away from home to join a summer camp for other children like him, with the end goal being to join the Psychonauts - a secret agency of psychics protecting the world from other ill meaning psychics. Raz starts his journey like any other kid a summer camp, around a campfire, where he then gets his own bunk to tuck in for the night. The next morning you go to your orientation boot camp, a little confused as to what you might actually be doing during psychic summer camp, burning things, lifting things with your mind. This is where the game takes a look into the mind, literally, and you go inside the coaches mind to complete your Basic Braining - yes, that is the real name lol. 

After getting your first badges, the game turns into a little semi open world, where you are free to explore the camp, searching for items to help you level up (like arrowheads, psi cards, etc.), and talking to the other campers before embarking on the next piece of your quest. Because you are so talented psychically, agents Sasha Nien and Milla Vodello begin to test your actual skill level in order for you to earn mor badges aka powers. The game naturally gate the powers in such a way where it feels impactful to earn, and immediately test you on the new skills you learned. Its classic game design 101. With Sasha you get the psi-blast and Milla you get levitation, my personal favorite, which unlocks movement tech for you to get in places you probably shouldn't be getting into.

Once you have all your powers, some of the children start to go missing, only to be found missing their brains. Sasha and Milla, get called for official psychonauts business - leaving the kids in the care of the old man aka Agent Cruller (a confused old fart who happens to be one of the founders of the Psychonauts). Raz and Cruller aren't convinced that things are okay, as Sasha and Milla haven't reported in hours at this point, so in order to find them, Raz and his new "girlfriend" Lili meet up to sneak over to the other side of the lake to find the "dentist" that was revealed to you in a dream, who was apparently held up in the old insane asylum - fitting.

From this point on, you feel like you are on a REAL Psychonauts mission. To cross the lake, you needed to befriend a giant fish, and to do so you had to free their mind from a control device put in place by a mystery Mr Evil Bad Guy™ through a, get this, Kaiju battle, where Raz is an invading Kauji on the fish city, and you have to defeat another "hero" in order to liberate the fish. Once thats done and you reach the asylum, you have to go into a few minds to not only get inside, but get up to the laboratory to rescue your friends. Im going to recall them in quick fire succession: 

To get inside, you must talk to the guard, who is looking for the milk man - inside his mind is a paranoia filled puzzle, finding a way through the labrinth of twisting world using disguises, to uncover the truth - HE IS THE MILK MAN...and his milk is delicious. Once inside, you must gather a disguise by helping 3 patients to get items in their possession to make you look like the dentist, in order to get past the orderly, who cant see very well. 1, you go inside an old opera singers mind - who has you solve a puzzle to play the right play at the right time, so that you can find the phantom...of the opera...yes...and defeat him to find her happy ending, and get the trophy that looks like the dentists claw. 2, you enter the mind of a painter, who is struggling with his past breakup and perceives himself as an unstoppable bull, whose rage is taking over his mind. To defeat the bull, you must first defeat his past - the wrestling team, and the cheer captain, who turns out was Mr Steal Your Girl™ - once you free him from his past, he paints you a picture of the dentists face, for you to use in your disguise. 3, was literally a game of Risk with Napoleon. You spend time helping peasants on the game board in order to gather enough troops to defeat napolean at his own game, in order to get the strait jacket. The fun this about this one, is Frank is a Bonaparte, and his struggle is with his own self doubt to be a great leader. With all those done, its time to get your friends back.

Finally, the end is in site! Raz reaches his friends and devises a plan to free them, the only thing standing in your way is Dr Loboto, and plot twist - the coach, who was plotting to steal brains to take over the world with his brain tanks! After blowing up the asylum - thanks milk man - you have to face the coaches evil mind tank - defeating it no sweat. But unfortunately, your minds become melded together and the real final boss appears. You have to help solve the coaches and Raz's daddy issues in a mess of a level of combined consciousnesses, with the circus and the butcher shop, all culminating in a bossfight where Raz takes on a mixture of both his and the coaches father...sheesh. Once free, the story resolves into Raz getting the recognition he deserves for being the hero, and finally becoming a true Psychonaut! 

I dont know why I recited the whole story here, but honestly its worth talking about because it was so fun to play through, and after not playing for nearly 20 years, so much of the story truely sticking with me from when I was a kid. What a wonderful world and characters, filled with timeless writing, and excellent world building. This is one brain worm of a story, and not one I will forget any time soon.

### Look & Feel

Despite its age, Psychonauts still looks and plays remarkably well. I will always say a timeless art style trumps realism any day, and it couldn't be more true here. The bright colors, the exaggerated body shapes, and the expressive characters all add To the sense of a fully realized world. I think that there's nothing really quite like it, and it really makes this game stand tall Against the test of time. I think that the level designs in the minds of the characters play a huge role in the game's sense of freshness, Even 20 years later. Luckily, The gameplay fully complements the look, Raz Controls fluidly throughout the world, and the bounciness/Floatiness of the movement makes you feel like an acrobat with extra levitation powers, which hits the nail right on the head. 

I really appreciated the amount of artistic liberty that was taken when creating this game, and the fact that every level is so visually distinct and still fitting within the design language of psychonauts is a real Testament to how much the developers cared about this IP. Even from my descriptions above in the story section I think it doesn't really give it justice to the mind bending art in game. Even 20 years ago the amount that they were able to achieve on such old and limited hardware is nothing short of a miracle. Take for example one of my favorite levels when you play Risk with Napoleon, You dynamically grow and shrink to go in and out of the game board. Maybe this is all smoke and mirrors, but the effect is really good. Another amazing example is the milkman level, where because of his paranoid mind the neighborhood is all twisted in on itself, having you walk on the ceilings walk around an ever rotating world, even before Super Mario Galaxy - Which was massively praised at the time, albeit years after psychonauts, As a pioneer of this 3D spinning world tech.

I don't think there's much else to say other than just looks and feels great, It makes me really excited to see what the sequel has in store. Imagine all the ideas that they couldn't complete due to a hardware limitation , now fully possible with the technology that we have now. That makes me really excited to keep playing, and would have driven me nuts waiting 20 years for that sequel.

### Sound & Music

Now here's where I think the game falls off a little bit. Not in any way that is a serious detriment, but more so in a way where it didn't stick out as much as the other elements of the game. The music is there, but I feel like it takes a back seat, so much so that it feels the most dated out of all the things in this game. It follows classic Game design music philosophy, But in an era when games started to become more blockbuster , with more songs and more dynamic ness between the music and the gameplay, A simple one to two minute loop of a level theme song kind of gets old. And I didn't think the music was good enough individually To make it really stand out , in fact there were moments where I just wish the music would stop playing. 

That brings me to the sound. In a similar way some of the sounds are really dated, to the point where I was getting a little bit irritated hearing the emotional baggage crying over and over and over again. The advancements in sound mixing and sound propagation for newer titles virtually eliminate this minor annoyances, Certainly an artifact of the past, But it definitely stood out amongst the Titan of other things going for this game. Everything here Fits exactly its purpose, but I feel like with so much effort in the visual style I feel like there should have been a little more effort in the musical style.

### Fun Factor

This game was a blast. I can't really elaborate further, All the levels are so distinct it made you want to keep going. I played through this game in three days with Riley sitting right next to me enjoying it. And when a game is fun enough to watch, It really shows how fun it is to play. There were not many moments in the game, especially for a platforming game mind you, That led me to frustration. Yeah some of the level choices can be a bit dated at times, and expect a lot of mechanical or precision prowess from the player, I think psychonauts balance of Difficult platforming An exploration/puzzle solving Really make the game a joy to play, and removes any of that potential frustration from having to Constantly platform. 

A lot of the fun here too comes from the writing, It really makes you feel engaged with the world And makes you want to interact with all the characters just to hear what they have to say. And isn't that what a fun game should make you want to do anyway? Psychonauts is just one of those games where you have a massive grin on your face the entire time you play it, and despite all the trouble to get it set up, and despite some 20 year old decisions that may or may not hold up super well, the majority of the game It's just fun. This is one of those games to make you fall in love with gaming. 

### Overall

I think Psychonauts Presented itself back into my life at an ideal time. At a time where gaming is feeling overly corporate, microtransactions and multiplayer games, competitiveness And c-suite fuckery Tailoring games to the broadest audience possible to make the most money, psychonauts presents itself as the reason why I play games. Because games are fun. Games are meant to engross you in a world, make you excited to want to explore it, make you happy to play it, consume your mind with the ideas that it presents, and most importantly To make you fall in love with as one of the most important mediums humans have created.

Psychonaut stands tall amongst other Titans of the industry as one of the most inventive and fun games ever created. There is a reason when I tell my friends I'm playing Psychonauts They've all heard of it. I think this is one of those games that deserves to be shared with those who aren't gamers, to show them this is what gaming stands for. It's no surprise to me that this game won Baftas, and it's no surprise to me that I had a blast playing it again Anne was able to remember so much from my childhood. I think this is a game that no one should miss, and I think everything I've said about it perfectly reflects that (maybe idk im not a critic).
