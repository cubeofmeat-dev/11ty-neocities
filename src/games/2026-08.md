# Stellar Blade


_August 10, 2026_

<p>
    <img class="img-logo snes-container no-padding" src="/images/sb-logo.png">
</p>

_May your memories live on forever._


### First Impression

Since seeing the trailers years and years ago, Stellar Blade has been on my wishlist. I never necessarily wanted to buy it on PS5, and lucky for me the game ended up launching on PC a couple years down the road. I had a hard time justifying the full price tag, even when it came to PC as I just wasn't convinced the gameplay was truly there, considering all the "gameplay" anyone really showed on social media were...risque to say the least. Is Stellar Blade a suggestive game? Yes, the camera often follows the protagonist Eve in such a way to make spectacle of her body, even when everything around you is just a typical action game setting. Is that the only thing the game should be known for? No, turns out that game that all of your friends were side eyeing you for ended up being a lot of fun, with a lot of skill expression, and a sci-fi world rich with story worth investing time into. 

### Story

To be honest, I wasn't really expecting much in terms of story in this game. It's always good to be pleasantly surprised, And I think stellar blade satisfied in that regard. I wasn't necessarily hooked until you find the human civilization a couple of missions in to the game. From there you meet a character that I think was particularly shady, and had me guessing their Motive for the entire game. Not like this game's going to win Any BAFTA awards for its story, I do feel like it was serviceable enough to provide a few thought provoking moments, and satisfying twist That resolves well in the end. Eve is a good protagonist, however I feel like the story was mostly driven by the side cast, and the missions that weren't integral to the main story were the ones I ended up caring about the most. There were moments for me where you truly felt like a guardian Angel, helping the people of Xion maintain a shred of hope in the dull world around them. I was happy with the main plot beats of the story, from traveling to the different regions, to the "dungeons" of each new region that gave more insight to what was happening in the world. Despite a lot of the story coming from notes and items found in the game, much like souls like games, I do feel the story presenting itself to you in a typical action RPG fashion works better here then solely relying on in game lore to drive the story. I'm glad there was a cast of characters to enjoy, and I'm glad to be surprised. 

### Look & Feel

Now say what you will about games from Korea, especially now since the gaming scene over there is really starting to take off, I do think they have... a look. I can't really put my finger on what that look necessarily is, But a variety of games from Korea have it, the first descendant, Aion, Lies of P, Stellar Blade. It's not a bad thing, It's just something I noticed as I started expanding what I played into South Korean games. Anyway, Stella Blade looks fantastic. Shift up deliberately chose to develop Stellar Blade on Unreal Engine 4 for the performance boost. In an era with so many Unreal Engine five games looking like a blurry mess, I really appreciate how crisp the picture is in stellar blade. Not to mention the game runs exceptionally well, despite my older PC And despite it Being a fairly new game. I think the character design is quite good, what people wear and how they look is reflective of The post apocalyptic sci fi look they're going for. The world's resigned quite well, and I really like the dystopian look kind of paired with that post apocalyptic look, It gives something unique that you don't really see in too many games - Probably the most notable for me that's a similar vibe is Rage which I played last year. I really loved the enemy design looks, I thought the Naytiba were all pretty unique - reminding me a lot of some floor forms from halo lol. Bosses were also quite good, although most of them were kind of based on creatures from Earth- Which is fitting for some of the reveals in the story. I started to get a little bit exhausted of the Naytiba towards the half way point, But Stellar Blade does a good job of introducing An interesting enemies throughout the length of my playthrough. 

I did really enjoy the gameplay. I feel like it felt similar to lies of P in a way, Where you're almost kind of meant to stand your ground, Sekiro style, And deflect a ton of attacks to find an opening. In fact the gameplay is designed around it, With a stagger system that rewards you with a large punishing attack after X amount of successful perfect parries. I did have to get an exo-spine to open that window a little more to be a little more forgiving, but the fact that the exo spine mechanic is here, allows for unique tailoring for different playstyles. Now as far as build variety goes, There are no other weapons you use outside of your original kit, So the build crafting all comes from which pieces of the kit you use the most, and building to min Max your playstyle. For example for me I really like to use the beta skills, and so I specked out different upgrades to assist me in getting more beta charge from killing enemies, Getting more beta charged for being hit, And going into a trance when using my beta energy so that I can keep using beta energy for free. This ended up sort of breaking the game towards the end, allowing me to stun lock bosses, and play really without needing to deflect at all, which was a ton of fun. Now while it was fun, I feel like I kind of found a broken build and potentially took some of the challenge out of the game. I do wish That stellar blade was a bit more challenging, especially because death is kind of weightless- Meaning when you die you just go back to the last checkpoint. No loss, no risk, No reward. It's one of those little things I think that set this on a stage a little bit lower than some of my other favorite games in this genre.

### Sound & Music

In general the sound and music in stellar Blade Varies from ear wormy to almost unnoticeable. There are so many musical tracks in the game that some of them, especially the bosses, kind of blur into one another. What I'm finding is that with a lot of action RPGS/souls likes I'm really starting to get exhausted of the Big Horn Big Boss motif. In a fantasy game like Elden Ring it works, but the tracks to me that stick out even amongst those games are the ones that are the most different. What's that stellar blade apart from a lot of these other games is that it has exploration music. Now it's all sung in Korean, So to me it ends up kind of just sounding like K drama soundtrack, But that doesn't mean it's bad. It fills the empty space in the large open areas, It makes it feel a little bit less lonely. What I did love was some of the safe room music, because it's just straight K pop- and the one that I used called blur Made me look forward to going back to save. A similar feeling to the safe room music in the Surge. There are a few tracks on the soundtrack that I really did enjoy, that were a little more EDM forward and fit the urgentness of when they appeared in the story. Sound is fine, don't really have many comments on that one outside of hearing Eve yell "wuaaaahhh" after nearly every combo did get a bit annoying at times, but I digress.

### Fun Factor

Stellar blade, fun factor I pretty much knew I was gonna have a good time with stellar blade. Action RPG sci-fi dystopian world and reasons to actually explore. On top of that put in combo based. and Perry based combat and I’m hooked, I think the mechanics of the game were gated in such a way to keep it interesting as you move throughout the story well also giving you more and more tools to play with classic game design done well and I really appreciated it for it. I had a lot of fun with the customization as well despite it being mostly swimsuits lol I was able to customize Eve into an evangelion like plug suit, which really cemented her character as part of the world for me. I think the bosses were fun and rewarding, but not as rewarding as other souls games because you just lose nothing on death so there is no fear for dying over and over again something in my mind tells me. that a lot of these small nuances in the first game can be ironed out and perfected in the second game and that makes me really excited to play the sequel.

### Overall

After about 40 hours, completing about 90%, and getting the true ending in the game- I think it's pretty fair to say that I had a fun time. I liked the combat, I liked the world and the story, I liked the side missions, And I like the traversal (despite being difficult to control at times, especially when entering the open world "boost" mode). It's not really a game that I wouldn't recommend to someone. There's a lot here for everyone. Do you like sci fi action games? Get it. Do you like female protagonists like Bayonetta? Get it. Do you like dystopian post apocalyptic settings? Get it. Do you like dressing up your main character, and tailoring them to be who they sound like in your mind? Get it. Stellar Blade is a great game Without a doubt. Clearly a lot of love was poured into this game and it shows. I'm really happy That shift up announced to sequel this year, Because I will be buying that day one.

## A-
