### First Impression

Ah, I remember this game. I remember it fondly. A journey through the mind, exploring ideas like depression, rage, mental blocking, paranoia, all disguised as a "kids" game. One thing I wish now in hindsight, is I wish I just had the original copy on the Xbox, because man...what a pain in the ass to get this working on modern hardware, especially the steam machine. Not only did I have to download and run scripts, I still never fixed the broken PC icons for the actions in game. Didn't really hinder the experience, but it did leave me a little worried for my run time with it. Luckily after the initial setup, it ran like a dream (despite the button icon issue), and for a 20 year old game, holds up remarkably well thanks to its amazing writing, art style, and timeless game design. There is a reason everyone has heard of Psychonauts, and once it was setup, I was itching to play.

### Story

Rasputin is the name, a gifted child psychic who ran away from home to join a summer camp for other children like him, with the end goal being to join the Psychonauts - a secret agency of psychics protecting the world from other ill meaning psychics. Raz starts his journey like any other kid a summer camp, around a campfire, where he then gets his own bunk to tuck in for the night. The next morning you go to your orientation boot camp, a little confused as to what you might actually be doing during psychic summer camp, burning things, lifting things with your mind. This is where the game takes a look into the mind, literally, and you go inside the coaches mind to complete your Basic Braining - yes, that is the real name lol. 

After getting your first badges, the game turns into a little semi open world, where you are free to explore the camp, searching for items to help you level up (like arrowheads, psi cards, etc.), and talking to the other campers before embarking on the next piece of your quest. Because you are so talented psychically, agents Sasha Nien and Milla Vodello begin to test your actual skill level in order for you to earn mor badges aka powers. The game naturally gate the powers in such a way where it feels impactful to earn, and immediately test you on the new skills you learned. Its classic game design 101. With Sasha you get the psi-blast and Milla you get levitation, my personal favorite, which unlocks movement tech for you to get in places you probably shouldn't be getting into.

Once you have all your powers, some of the children start to go missing, only to be found missing their brains. Sasha and Milla, get called for official psychonauts business - leaving the kids in the care of the old man aka Agent Cruller (a confused old fart who happens to be one of the founders of the Psychonauts). Raz and Cruller aren't convinced that things are okay, as Sasha and Milla haven't reported in hours at this point, so in order to find them, Raz and his new "girlfriend" Lili meet up to sneak over to the other side of the lake to find the "dentist" that was revealed to you in a dream, who was apparently held up in the old insane asylum - fitting.

From this point on, you feel like you are on a REAL Psychonauts mission. To cross the lake, you needed to befriend a giant fish, and to do so you had to free their mind from a control device put in place by a mystery Mr Evil Bad Guy™ through a, get this, Kaiju battle, where Raz is an invading Kauji on the fish city, and you have to defeat another "hero" in order to liberate the fish. Once thats done and you reach the asylum, you have to go into a few minds to not only get inside, but get up to the laboratory to rescue your friends. Im going to recall them in quick fire succession: 

To get inside, you must talk to the guard, who is looking for the milk man - inside his mind is a paranoia filled puzzle, finding a way through the labrinth of twisting world using disguises, to uncover the truth - HE IS THE MILK MAN...and his milk is delicious. Once inside, you must gather a disguise by helping 3 patients to get items in their possession to make you look like the dentist, in order to get past the orderly, who cant see very well. 1, you go inside an old opera singers mind - who has you solve a puzzle to play the right play at the right time, so that you can find the phantom...of the opera...yes...and defeat him to find her happy ending, and get the trophy that looks like the dentists claw. 2, you enter the mind of a painter, who is struggling with his past breakup and perceives himself as an unstoppable bull, whose rage is taking over his mind. To defeat the bull, you must first defeat his past - the wrestling team, and the cheer captain, who turns out was Mr Steal Your Girl™ - once you free him from his past, he paints you a picture of the dentists face, for you to use in your disguise. 3, was literally a game of Risk with Napoleon. You spend time helping peasants on the game board in order to gather enough troops to defeat napolean at his own game, in order to get the strait jacket. The fun this about this one, is Frank is a Bonaparte, and his struggle is with his own self doubt to be a great leader. With all those done, its time to get your friends back.

Finally, the end is in site! Raz reaches his friends and devises a plan to free them, the only thing standing in your way is Dr Loboto, and plot twist - the coach, who was plotting to steal brains to take over the world with his brain tanks! After blowing up the asylum - thanks milk man - you have to face the coaches evil mind tank - defeating it no sweat. But unfortunately, your minds become melded together and the real final boss appears. You have to help solve the coaches and Raz's daddy issues in a mess of a level of combined consciousnesses, with the circus and the butcher shop, all culminating in a bossfight where Raz takes on a mixture of both his and the coaches father...sheesh. Once free, the story resolves into Raz getting the recognition he deserves for being the hero, and finally becoming a true Psychonaut! 

I dont know why I recited the whole story here, but honestly its worth talking about because it was so fun to play through, and after not playing for nearly 20 years, so much of the story truely sticking with me from when I was a kid. What a wonderful world and characters, filled with timeless writing, and excellent world building. This is one brain worm of a story, and not one I will forget any time soon.

### Look & Feel

Despite its age, Psychonauts still looks and plays remarkably well. I will always say a timeless art style trumps realism any day, and it couldn't be more true here. The bright colors, the exaggerated body shapes, and the expressive characters all add To the sense of a fully realized world. I think that there's nothing really quite like it, and it really makes this game stand tall Against the test of time. I think that the level designs in the minds of the characters play a huge role in the game's sense of freshness, Even 20 years later. Luckily, The gameplay fully complements the look, Raz Controls fluidly throughout the world, and the bounciness/Floatiness of the movement makes you feel like an acrobat with extra levitation powers, which hits the nail right on the head. 

I really appreciated the amount of artistic liberty that was taken when creating this game, and the fact that every level is so visually distinct and still fitting within the design language of psychonauts is a real Testament to how much the developers cared about this IP. Even from my descriptions above in the story section I think it doesn't really give it justice to the mind bending art in game. Even 20 years ago the amount that they were able to achieve on such old and limited hardware is nothing short of a miracle. Take for example one of my favorite levels when you play Risk with Napoleon, You dynamically grow and shrink to go in and out of the game board. Maybe this is all smoke and mirrors, but the effect is really good. Another amazing example is the milkman level, where because of his paranoid mind the neighborhood is all twisted in on itself, having you walk on the ceilings walk around an ever rotating world, even before Super Mario Galaxy - Which was massively praised at the time, albeit years after psychonauts, As a pioneer of this 3D spinning world tech.

I don't think there's much else to say other than just looks and feels great, It makes me really excited to see what the sequel has in store. Imagine all the ideas that they couldn't complete due to a hardware limitation , now fully possible with the technology that we have now. That makes me really excited to keep playing, and would have driven me nuts waiting 20 years for that sequel.

### Sound & Music

Now here's where I think the game falls off a little bit. Not in any way that is a serious detriment, but more so in a way where it didn't stick out as much as the other elements of the game. The music is there, but I feel like it takes a back seat, so much so that it feels the most dated out of all the things in this game. It follows classic Game design music philosophy, But in an era when games started to become more blockbuster , with more songs and more dynamic ness between the music and the gameplay, A simple one to two minute loop of a level theme song kind of gets old. And I didn't think the music was good enough individually To make it really stand out , in fact there were moments where I just wish the music would stop playing. 

That brings me to the sound. In a similar way some of the sounds are really dated, to the point where I was getting a little bit irritated hearing the emotional baggage crying over and over and over again. The advancements in sound mixing and sound propagation for newer titles virtually eliminate this minor annoyances, Certainly an artifact of the past, But it definitely stood out amongst the Titan of other things going for this game. Everything here Fits exactly its purpose, but I feel like with so much effort in the visual style I feel like there should have been a little more effort in the musical style.

### Fun Factor

This game was a blast. I can't really elaborate further, All the levels are so distinct it made you want to keep going. I played through this game in three days with Riley sitting right next to me enjoying it. And when a game is fun enough to watch, It really shows how fun it is to play. There were not many moments in the game, especially for a platforming game mind you, That led me to frustration. Yeah some of the level choices can be a bit dated at times, and expect a lot of mechanical or precision prowess from the player, I think psychonauts balance of Difficult platforming An exploration/puzzle solving Really make the game a joy to play, and removes any of that potential frustration from having to Constantly platform. 

A lot of the fun here too comes from the writing, It really makes you feel engaged with the world And makes you want to interact with all the characters just to hear what they have to say. And isn't that what a fun game should make you want to do anyway? Psychonauts is just one of those games where you have a massive grin on your face the entire time you play it, and despite all the trouble to get it set up, and despite some 20 year old decisions that may or may not hold up super well, the majority of the game It's just fun. This is one of those games to make you fall in love with gaming. 

### Overall

I think Psychonauts Presented itself back into my life at an ideal time. At a time where gaming is feeling overly corporate, microtransactions and multiplayer games, competitiveness And c-suite fuckery Tailoring games to the broadest audience possible to make the most money, psychonauts presents itself as the reason why I play games. Because games are fun. Games are meant to engross you in a world, make you excited to want to explore it, make you happy to play it, consume your mind with the ideas that it presents, and most importantly To make you fall in love with as one of the most important mediums humans have created.

Psychonaut stands tall amongst other Titans of the industry as one of the most inventive and fun games ever created. There is a reason when I tell my friends I'm playing Psychonauts They've all heard of it. I think this is one of those games that deserves to be shared with those who aren't gamers, to show them this is what gaming stands for. It's no surprise to me that this game won Baftas, and it's no surprise to me that I had a blast playing it again Anne was able to remember so much from my childhood. I think this is a game that no one should miss, and I think everything I've said about it perfectly reflects that (maybe idk im not a critic).
