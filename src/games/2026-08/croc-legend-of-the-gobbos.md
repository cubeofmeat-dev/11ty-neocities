# Croc: Legend of the Gobbos

_August 17, 2026_

<p>
    <img class="img-logo snes-container no-padding" src="/images/croc-logo.png">
</p>

_Yazoo! Shazam!_

### First Impression

A wave of nostalgia started by a great opening sequence in a very typical 90s bedroom. I was ready to rehash some early childhood memories with Croc. Upon entering I could tell a lot of love went into the original game, and all the extras to give you a glimpse into the development is such a nice touch. It really feels like this game being ported over to modern hardware was to be able to have more people share the experience. The modernized movement was a super nice touch, and really makes jumping in to play this easier than ever before.

### Story

Theres not much in terms of story here, like many 90s games. Croc is adopted by the Gobbos, and spends his childhood with them growing up. Suddenly, Mr Evil Bad Guy™ shows up and kidnaps all the Gobbos and its up to Croc to save them! You then spend the rest of the game romping through a variety of levels saving said Gobbos, and thwarting any bosses that get thrown your way.

### Look & Feel

Not much was changed in terms of looks. Croc gets a nice HD texturing, better lighting effects, and no more texture warble, but overall everything looks nearly identical - really selling the nostalgia. The movement being changed from the tank controls to omnidirectional with the joystick was probably one of the biggest improvements, and I can tell some of the sections must have been agonizing without it back in the day. If you played Croc on the PS1, playing now feels like a treat, instead of like a chore.

### Sound & Music

Now for some reason, I dont remember Croc having any music when I was a kid. Not sure if I turned it off, or if my disc was scratched or what, but I swear it was all atmospheric, which too me really identified Croc in my mind. However, I was actually disappointed this was not the case! Not that the music is bad, just compared to other games at the time, Crocs music is really...underwhelming? I even turned it off for a spell because I felt it was very repetitive at times - an often hallmark of some of these older games.

### Fun Factor

Croc is fun, and its fun to rehash some memories by playing through it again on modern hardware. I do think there was a reason I never beat it as a kid, and the same reason why I am not beating it (at least right now) as an adult. The games level design is just old. A lot of precise timing, positioning, and unforgiving checkpoints to artificially lengthen Crocs otherwise 3-4 hour playtime. Especially without save states, the frustration of losing all your lives to a difficult section and having to start the level over is not something someone who plays games for fun has time to truly spend time getting over. I know its an old game, and I think the oldness doesn't do it any favors in this instance.

### Overall

I wish I could've seen Croc through to the end. Its obvious it was, and still is a labor of love by the developers. I think that despite everything, Croc is showing its nearly 30 year old age, and often not in good ways. This is great fun to have for the first few worlds, but towards the end of the game, the difficulty spike can be overly frustrating, and I deemed it not worth my time past a few hours of fun. My rating is much lower than I wanted so I am going to also give a secondary rating on how I felt actually playing except for the end. I feel like this with multiple games, they just leave a bad end taste in my mouth that affects the score. Croc IS worth playing, especially if you played when you were younger, and this is the way to play it outside of the original hardware.
