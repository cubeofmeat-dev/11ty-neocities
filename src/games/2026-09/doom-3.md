# Doom 3

_September 5, 2026_

		<img class="img-logo snes-container no-padding" src="/images/doom3-logo.png">
</p>

_The Devil is real, I know. I built his cage._

### First Impression

I remembered really liking Doom 3 despite all the hate. Yeah its not your traditional Doom game in the sense of the run and gun, chugging metal music, invincible feeling. Its darker, grittier, grounded, and took a major chance trying to bring the series into a new era of gaming. Since then, id has made a complete 180 back to their roots, and dropping the moody  tangent back to good old Doom guy blowing the face off everything that moves. But, for what it is - I really like it. 

Doom 3 slots right in with the FEARs, and other survival horrors that were on the rage in the early 2000s, and with the power of idTech3 they were really able to nail the oppressive darkness feeling, better than most other games of the time. The rely on the uncertainty of whats in the dark, while giving you the equalizing device to actually fight back - much of the reason why I like it so much. As someone who is not a horror fan, give me the ability to change the outcome, and suddenly Im the monster in the closet.

### Story

I think rating the Doom stories is kind of unfair unless you are a super fan who dives super deep into the lore implications that Doom Guy != Doom Marine, and the alternate time line, hell on earth blah blah blah. I am indeed not that fan, and Doom to me has always been this "level based, chop till ya drop" type game, and Doom 3s departure from that makes it really special in my eyes. 

Essentially a wild goose chase through the mars facility, Doom Marine starts his first day on the job in between a crazy scientist(Mr Evil Bad Guy™ - more on him later) being questioned by an Earth senator and his right hand man aka your bosses. Mr Evil Bad Guy™ is running rogue attempting to solve the power crisis by figuring out teleportation, and in doing so finds a rift to hell. They look into hell as a power source, and end up unleashing unknown horrors upon mars by experimenting with artifacts humanity should have never found in the first place. 

While you're out on an errand run, the whole facility gets ransacked by the souls of the damned, turning innocent workers into zombies, opening rifts to hell, and letting all manners of demons into the mars base. Now comes your mission - find Mr Evil Bad Guy™, and end this madness! You are lead all through the mars base, aiding random scientists, finding datapads for door combinations, and making your way through room by room all the way to delta complex where the main teleportation experiments were taking place.

Once you arrive, you see your first glimpse into hell, and get the single handed most important item in the game - the Soul Cube. Without it, you couldnt beat the final boss (you have to take it but stay with me here), and you wouldnt be able to stop Mr Evil Bad Guy™ from unleashing hell onto earth and enslaving all of humanity for his own gain. Very evil indeed.

I enjoyed that the story was straight forward, and with Doom there doesnt really need to be much of anything, which is why I called it a bit unfair. Its just enough plot to move the game while you go about in your demon killing ways. While Doom 3 was inherantly less action oriented than the other titles, that doesnt mean it was a story based game, or even a survival horror. This is still a shooter, through and through - and I think the kinda average story aligns it perfectly with the rest of the series. id has always put function over form.

### Look & Feel

Personally I'm a fan, in fact a huge fan, of this early to mid 2000s hardcore game look. I know lots of people think it's drab maybe a little too muted colors, but it's something distinctly unique from the era, and it was the era that I grew up playing these games. I was into my teens and getting my hands on an M rated game was rare, but when I did it was games like Doom 3. For some reason my parents viewed shooting zombies and shooting aliens as far less harmful than shooting people, which is real and I get it. And in that I played a lot of games that had this kind of look, which I hold an endearing Regard even if it is with some rose tinted glasses.

Doom 3's look Isn't really Unique per se, but what it does do in terms of the ambience, the lighting, the grotesqueness of it all- is all reminiscent of survival horror games, which that type of look is typically reserved for them. Doom 3 does a really good job of bringing that look into a first person shooter, and really making it work for them. I really loved the use of dynamic lighting, even if some people really did not like having to take out the flashlight and put it away to use your gun.

I think the original mechanics work extremely well, despite some of the critical reception surrounding it. Having to use your flashlight to illuminate the extreme darkness in some settings, put it away, and take out your gun to blast whatever the fuck is hiding in the shadows, was a deliberate decision. I loved it so much that I often only turn the flashlight on when I absolutely need it (Because of the BFG Edition they add in the famous duct tape mod where you can just keep your flashlight on your gun at all times), And shoot in the darkness as the game originally intended. When there are massive firefights to be had there's enough lighting in the rooms to where you feel like you're not handicapped by the darkness. I really like the deliberate choices here, And to me really add to the feel of the game.

Now it's Doom, The guns feel good and weighty, the enemies are fun to fight albeit a little dumb, and you really feel like a one man army by the end. I think Doom 3 is no exception to that either, but I do think people get held up on the wrong things about this game, and the things that Doom 3 does really unique, it does them very well.

### Sound & Music

I gave sound and music a slightly lower score here because there really isn't much of anything to go on based on that especially in the music department. Long gone are the metal riffs and chugging from previous iterations, In favor for a darker more ambient approach. Which when looked at in a vacuum fit really well all things considered, but when you look at Doom then you start to get some of the critics takes, making Doom 3 a bit of a black sheep. 

I think the sound is fine and I think what it does really well is the 3D aspect of it. You can often echolocate an enemy in the complete darkness because the sound is really accurate, and that's something not really talked about a lot either in this game. For 2004 that's insanely impressive. On the other end, some of the choices that they made for the weapons feel really bad. The Assault rifle doesn't sound powerful at all, The shotgun sounds extremely powerful, The plasma rifle (as powerful as it actually is) Sounds like a kids play gun, All tied together with the moans and groans of demons and zombies, It makes the sound mix sound a little weird at times. Something uniquely Doom 3 - I feel like if I was blindfolded and played the sounds from this game I would immediately know what I was listening to - However there are some choices Let's just make this a really weird bag all around.

### Fun Factor

Like all the other Dooms, 3 is no exception to fun. Once I actually started playing I couldn't put it down. I really loved Some of the deliberate choices that I made during my playthrough, in using the flashlight sparingly, using the shotgun more and barrel stuffing the fuck out of everyone, and spending some time looking around for PDAs So that I can get access to better guns a little bit earlier than intended in the play through. Even though this was a revisit for me I still had a lot of fun playing. The guns felt good, The combat was surprisingly quick if you're actually sprinting, And what little bit of exploration is actually in the game felt pretty rewarding (Even if all it was was an excuse to use the controller to type numbers into the keypad in real time - Which for some reason I just really love in this game it doesn't take you out of the moment and it feels like You are tangibly there). 

Like other dooms, You're a huge powerhouse towards the end of the game - And that power fantasy feels good, that's why everyone plays these. And I certainly had a good time doing so, even if...3 is just that much different from the series.

### Overall

I think my time with Doom 3 was well deserved. While being baked in nostalgia, Viewing the game through rose tinted glasses is exactly how you're supposed to play these old games. What's the point in going back and being overly critical when there's no reason to be overly critical in the first place. Sure some things don't age well, and other things are more frustrating than they're worth when revisiting, But Doom 3 is neither of those. I think it ages remarkably well despite the time since its release. I think that many people are overly critical on the decisions that id Made intentionally when designing the game. Hell, It's one of the reasons why I keep revisiting, And I think it's going to remain a reason To revisit in the future - Even if it is for a little nostalgia... What's wrong with that?
