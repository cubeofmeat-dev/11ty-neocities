### First Impression

This was pretty random. I got these a little while back during a Humble Bundle (go figure), for international women's day or something. It was celebrating female protagonists, and so I picked it up to support and get some new games on the low low. I've played around with these titles in my past, mostly at some of my girl cousins and some family friends, who's Gameboy games were unsurprisingly girl oriented. Shantae is one of those titles, and I know the IP well enough to instantly recognize her as a character. I figured what the heck, I'm just sitting around bored anyway scrolling, might as well just do some throw back to some old DS era games, and have a good time doing do. 

I was pleasantly surprised with the overall package of Risky's Revenge. Sure it's an old DS title - but that doesn't it can't be a good time. I was surprised in many ways on how it holds up, and in other ways, not surprised as all. Considering this is a nearly 20 year old game, I'm taking the experience I had with a grain of salt.

### Story

I mean...it's an early DS game. Like the Mario's and other platformers of the era, there really wasn't much of story to go with it. I do applaud Shantae though because it's not really bare bones, just pretty simple. It does exactly what you'd expect a older portable kids game story to do. Text bubbles, small cutscenes, cute silly animations, and cute silly characters. Obviously that is the vibe the devs were going for and they hit the nail on the head, but it's not a story to write home about, and mostly a plot device. 

Instead of my trademarked™ Mr Evil Bad Guy™, there is now a Mrs Evil Bad Girl™! Who progresses the plot by stealing an artifact, and you are lead on a wild goose chase to get 3 seals to unveil the artifacts true power, and during your adventure you meet a cast of characters who are guarding those seals. Once you have them and all your powers, its time to showdown with the big baddy - ultimately losing all your powers but guess what? Now the world is left in peace, and the previous half-genie Shantae is now full human, yay!

### Look & Feel

Since I played this on a Steam Deck, it's not fully fair to judge the "feel" of this game, and I 100% mean physical feel. Its evident that some of the menus in game would have been really convenient if I had another screen. Part of the unfortunate of not having the original media, but it was serviceable fine enough. Just a minor gripe.

To the actual look and feel, I felt right at home. The gameplay and graphics were right inline with how I figured they would be for a game like this. Great pixel art, great backgrounds, and intuitive simple interactions with the game as a whole. The gameplay was reasonably tight, and I didn't ever feel like I was fighting the controls. I'm always a sucker for great pixel art, and going back to these older portables really show me how the art was lost over time. Really enjoyed looking at all the nice backgrounds and super vibrant color palette. 

### Sound & Music

I think this game is probably just under par in the music and sound department. A lot of the songs in this OST are hiphop vibe, while just being goofy enough to remind you you're still playing a kids game, which I did really enjoy. The music didn't overstate the game, and was just memorable enough to start humming some tunes towards the end of the game. 

The sound on the other hand was less so interesting. A lot of repeated sharp noises, that did get annoying after time. I can see how my parents were irritated at some of the games were were playing as kids, imagine having to listen to it on repeat while your stupid kids struggle to beat the game for MONTHS XD.

### Fun Factor

I enjoyed this one. It snuck into some couch and bed time when I really needed it most. I was feeling down the last week of September, and just needed something I didn't really have to think too much about, and can finish in a relatively short amount of time. The gameplay was simple enough to have a little depth in your choices, mostly collecting materials for some key upgrades to make yourself virtually unstoppable, and I guess the choices I made early on in what to invest in, really made the game a breeze.

Combat was good enough and bosses were varied enough to make you really want to continue. Although with these older games, not everything comes naturally, and towards the last 1/3 of the game they introduce some artificial game lengthening "quests" to pretty much make you comb the map once again, just to get into the final area. Didn't like that as much, but a quick search of where to do remedied that - I wasn't about to waste my time like a little kid haha.

On that note, likely more fun to be had as a child - this is indeed for them anyway, but playing as a GAM (Grown Ass Man) - I felt like I did enjoy my time enough to finish all the way through, despite some age.

### Overall

In an effort to reduce my doom scrolling, I just imagined what I did when I was a younger lad. Played games...like...all the time. With my SP and later my DS, boredom was never really a thing. Sure there were moments, but I never really had to just ingest nonsense over and over again - I can structure my boredom, and actually achieve something - like beating Shantae! 

I tend to have these games come up during some key moments in my life where things get stressful. Often, in moments of weakness, we seek out our past to put things into perspective, and to also enjoy in the every powerful drug of nostalgia. Thanks to Shantae for really kicking off a mid 2000s revisit for me this October.

This game really put into perspective that things don't have to be the best thing every to enjoy it. Just slow down, and play a silly little something to take the stress away and have fun.
