### First Impression

A bigger and better experience in almost every way, Shantae and the Pirate's Curse delivers on nearly everything I thought was a miss from the first title. The story is more intriguing, with more animated cutscenes, some voice acting, better music, better level design, and set pieces to boot! When you think of a first impression making a good one, Pirates Curse really comes out swinging!

I was immediately blown away by the improved cutscenes to set the stage for what was taking place immediately after the closing of the previous title, and I love when a game wastes no time bringing you back up to speed. You dont need to know what was happening before, but it makes the opening sequence that much sweeter. 

Even if you just recently lost your genie powers from the last game, Pirates Curse turns more towards a traditional MetroidVania this go round, ditching the really cool multi-layered world you can traverse through (major bummor for me), in favor of a traditional interconnected set of worlds, where you explore until the map fills up with blue boxes (MetroidVania gamers know what I'm talking about). I think Shantae feels really good in this format, and my enjoyment was set in stone from the very beginning.

### Story

While I wasn’t really impressed with the story of risky, revenge being that it was a simple DS game with a simple plot just to drive things around. I can say I enjoyed the story of pirates curse a little better than the previous iteration. I think that the story I’ll be it still pretty simple made me care a little bit more about the characters. I was interacting with as opposed to feeling like I was on a fetch quest the entire time.

Risky boots is back! But she’s no longer Mrs. evil bad girl, and instead, the both of you set off on a quest to find dens of evil around the kingdom and destroy them so that the pirate king cannot come back. You spend your time swashbuckling around different islands, completing quests specific to that island, and helping the citizens of the town when you come back to Port. The main driver of the story being those dens of evil is exactly how you power up and gain new abilities as well. 

After you find all the dens of evil, and power up with all the Riskys old pirate gear, you must go back to your hometown for the final face-off against the pirate King. Using the dark magic that you collected naturally while exploring the game, you bring it to the pirate king in exchange for risky freedom. He obliges, but you were proving that risky is always one step ahead in which she had a hunch that giving the genie magic would ultimately give Shantae back her long, lost powers. And with her powers back, you stick it to the pirate king saving the town from darkness and saving risky’s fate.

### Look & Feel

Now while this iteration of Shantae doesn’t really do anything super new in terms of look the overall feel to the game is vastly improved. In my opinion. Suddenly, you have movement abilities awesome new powers, a new abilities all to replace what you lost because you’re no longer a half Jeannie think the games fluidity is largely attributed. To your inability to use magic and the game design is having to be creative to give you new gear to truly make the world feel as if you were discovering it and just like any great Metroid game, discovering the world and coming back to previous sections to find that you now have an ability that grant you access that you didn’t think you had access to it’s really rewarding not to mention the reward of the movement mechanics of which specifically when you get the dash scimitar was some of my favorite movement tech since super Metroid because it’s basically speed booster. Now not only is the exploration good on its own with all of your abilities, but it’s made even better with a major bop of a soundtrack.

### Sound & Music

One of the things I noticed first about Pirates curse is that the soundtrack receive the major overhaul complicity of the DS version is mostly gone and instead you have a head bopping soundtrack that makes every time you revisit a old area just as fun as visiting it for the first time add something. I don’t think James‘s got enough credit for when they make the music stand out and be memorable on its own that means my favorite track. Is this one when you go to line Island, which is a great indication on the rest of the game soundtrack.

### Fun Factor

I think a lot of people discount these games as being a silly kid game about a genie in a pirate and that’s true. No one should discount a kids game as not being fun. pirates revenge was the opposite of that. I almost couldn’t put it down. Once I started playing, and even if in it lasts only roughly 7 hours of playtime, everything in the game made me wanna just continue on. The movement, the abilities and the exploration felt rewarding enough to not want to take a break and all of the other factors here really made this version special to me.

Every dungeon and every boss leads to a new ability that unlocks more potential for each of the previous islands and dungeons. It really gives a reason to explore and remember where to use your new powers or gear rather in this game (I wish you had the ability to mark the map so that you remembered more easily where you’d have to go back to). Discovering new routes when you find a new piece of pirate gear is just as satisfying as it is in any metro game, and I think pirates curse stands up there with the other MetroidVanias that I really enjoy, and I think I’ll be revisiting this one in the future sometime.

### Overall

Shantae and the Pirates curse was extremely fun. And even if it isn’t the most unique or perfectly executed Metroid Vania, I think it has plenty of charm to make me want to recommend it to new time players. Hell, I would even recommend this one over some of the other titles in the series I enjoyed it that much. And who knows maybe I’ll end up joining the other titles, especially the later ones more, but have a sneaking suspicion that this version will continue to be one of my favorites.
