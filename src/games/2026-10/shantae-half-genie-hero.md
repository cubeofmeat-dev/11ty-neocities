# Shantae: Half-Genie Hero

### First Impression

I was really riding the high coming off of Pirates curse. What a great Metroidvania. I was really happy with the game play, the loop of discovering items and taking them back to older areas, finding new ways through what you previously thought was closed off. There were cool new set pieces that you get to experience, and everything just flowed so well together. 

Now coming over to Half Genie Hero, I was expecting big things, and they didn’t really come to fruition for me. From first look, I could really tell that this was a modernization attempt. And unfortunately, really didn't stick with me. The old pixel sprite art style was gone in favor of a new “hand-drawn” cartoony look that's popular with a lot of new indie games today. Pair it with 3D backgrounds, modern rendering techniques and lightning it makes sense. Obviously, the developers were looking for something more modern, easier to do, and more sustainable long term for expansions and squeals. But man, I do really love the 2D sprites more than this art style.

I can't say that didn't have fun with Half-Genie Hero, though. However, you don't get that big set piece right off the bat. What you get instead is a kind of get a lame, soft intro. “Oh, your genie powers are back yay!” Time to save the Genie realm for some reason. From that into (or lack there of) I wasn't super excited to play, which is a pretty big bummer. So what, maybe the art style changed, but I'd probably be getting the same experience that I had been used to getting for Risky's Revenge and Pirate's Curse, right?

Starting up the first level after the intro something was off to me. No map. After playing the 1st level, I felt like This is this is pretty cool, the city is under attack, lets run through and fend off. Plays just like the games of the past. You head back to town after defeating Risky again, and go speak to Sky about heading out towards your next objective. You need to find something again back in town. She asks you if you're ready to go and you get on her giant bird and fly into the sky!  Then you’re greeted by a map screen. A linear map screen mind you - not a big deal, Pirates Revenge had a map where you went to your locations first before the game opened up the area for you to explore. No problem, the game just started anyway.

When you head back again to the first level, it's as linear as it gets - it’s just the same level again, but _maybe_ you have another ability to find an alternate path. But the level plays and finishes the exact same way every time. There's plenty of going back and forth to these levels, but you'd complete the level in the linear fashion every single time. There's no backtracking, there's no experimentation. It just felt really lazy. The world felt disconnected. Turns out I wouldn’t be getting another Metroidvania, further making it more disappointing, and coming from Pirates Curse, what a weird design decision, and a departure from the other games in the series.

### Story

Now, the story in these games, are typically nothing to write home about. Specifically because there's not really much going on. I would say the story is perfectly serviceable again, while not having anything to actually remember past the levels, and game play. I've felt this way about the other games as well, so not really surprised there. 

Shantae has gotten her Genie powers back, and now there's an issue with the genie realm. Of course, Risky Boots is at it again, trying to steal power from the genie realm, and Shantae is on a mission to stop that, and maybe get to see her mom again. What the story basically boils down to is, Shantae gets sent on a fetch quest, much like the other games, to gather pieces of equipment to build this magic polarizing ray her Uncle is setting up to reverse the polls and free the genie realm and dispel all the dark magic tamping into the genie realm. That's basically it. in terms of story. You go, you save the day, and job well done. A little underwhelming, and not something that I really remember all that much, even if it's just been a couple of weeks since playing and writing this review.

### Look & Feel

I can attest that the feel of this game is like all the other Shantae’s that I’ve played previously. Tight action platforming, fair and simple combat, and shapeshifting abilities to solve puzzles and platforming challenges throughout the game. Nothing much to say on that front. The other games in the series have already set that precedence - if the developers did nothing with the prior games mechanics, arguably the game would still feel great. Now I have strong opinions on the look, and of course its all personal preference.

Still, I said before and I will say again - I really wish the developers stuck with the pixelated art style of the previous games. There is something to me so charming and nostalgic about that style, and the departure from it deeply saddens me. Not that the new art style is bad, and by contrast of my disappointment, I actually like the direction in some instances - mostly thinking about the 3D mixed with 2D sequences, that contrast does looks really good. 

Its obvious the people who worked on this game did a lot to capture the art of the original games, and by shifting to the drawn art style, they actually align more closely to the characters art when they are speaking in the older games. In that regard, the art style is more closely resemblant of the world of Shantae - and I think I may be in the minority of people who actually prefer the lower fidelity look of the pixel art.

C’est La Vie - Thats why we all have opinions.

### Sound & Music

I cant say you can color me impressed by the overall sound in this game. It’s fine, and there are a few memorable tunes that kept the beat of the game while I was playing. I think the most noticeable update is Shantae is much more vocal. Slowly increasing over the course of the games, Shantae graduated from the simple interjection of character names, to more full interjections. E.g. “Risky Boots!” turns into “Risky Boots, I knew you’d be behind this!”. A Small touch, but a welcome one for sure.

For the music I didn’t really have anything that stood out to me to the point of really remembering. Everything now has a more generic house music vibe to it, which isn’t really a bad thing, but what modern games fail to capture a lot of the times is a unique melody. Ones that make the soundtrack stick in your brain and remember. I think for me, the lack or ear worms took the overall score down a bit.

One of my favorite pieces, and honestly favorite levels to the entire game was the Mermaid factory level - which has this upbeat industrial sound, that fit right in with the vibe of the level. Girls are being stolen and forced to transform into Mermaids LOL.

### Fun Factor

I did have fun with the game, but like the very first entry in this Shantae journey - I felt like something was missing. All the ingredients were there for a great game, and yet it just fell short for me. The transformation was back, but it really didn’t revolutionize the game in the way I waas hoping it actually would. Instead of it unlocking a new way through an area, your transformations only really mattered to get extras for the true ending it felt like.

Having the levels be so linear was a big hinderance to the transformation mechanic in general. Like any good MetroidVania, a new transformation can fundamentally change the way you approach the games mechanics - and Half-Genie Hero never really delivered on that front. Much of the mechanics felt forced in a way that didn't feel good. It didn't really feel good to be forced through the levels again with no real way to explore differently outside of a minor puzzle in a room that relied on your abilities you gained to unlock something further. Getting to those ultimately meant skipping the sections entirely, just to get to the one minor puzzle, just to get another item that ultimately didn't really have much impact on your playtime. Objectively, the game was fun - but you rarely had a reason to keep playing, and even more rarely you have a reason to revisit.

### Overall

While objectively Half-Genie Hero was fun, I felt less connected to this entry as I did the other entries in the series. I didn’t feel a real need to go and discover how the levels interconnected because they felt more isolated and straightforward as the other entries in the series which was a real draw back.

While I did end up playing the whole thing, and enjoying the experience, there is no real reason to go back and play years later. I think the taste that was left in my mouth after finishing was less “I cant wait to replay this in the future”, and more like “I hope the next game is even better than this”. 

I felt this way about Risky’s Revenge - as if a sequel could correct all the weird feelings I had about this game, and truely take it to the next level. Seven Sirens is up next, and I hope it is the case. I want all of these games to continue to be made, and while a cult classic, it is important for games that are just good/okay to be made, so that something great can come in its place.
